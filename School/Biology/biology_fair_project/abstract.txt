The purpose of this experiment was to find out which liquid would keep apple
slices from turning brown the longest. When an apple is cut and exposed to air,
it starts turning brown because of a reaction with oxygen. My hypothesis was
that lemon juice would work the best because it contains vitamin C, which helps
slow down browning.

For this experiment, I cut an apple into five equal slices and dipped each one
into a different liquid: water, lemon juice, saltwater, vinegar, and milk. Each
slice was dipped for 30 seconds and then placed on a plate. I checked the slices
every 15 minutes for 90 minutes and recorded how much they browned on a scale
from 1 to 5. I also recorded any changes in their color and texture.

After 90 minutes, the slices dipped in water, lemon juice, and milk had no
visible browning and stayed at a score of 1. The saltwater slice had some
browning and reached a score of 2.5, while vinegar had the most browning with
a score of 4. I also noticed that the water and milk slices became rough,
while the saltwater and vinegar slices became soggy. The lemon juice slice
stayed smooth but turned slightly lime-green.

Overall, my hypothesis was partially correct. Lemon juice worked the best
because it prevented browning without making the apple rough or soggy. However,
water and milk also prevented browning during the experiment. From these
results, I learned that different liquids can affect both the color and texture
of apples. This information could be useful when preparing apple slices ahead
of time for lunches or snacks.
